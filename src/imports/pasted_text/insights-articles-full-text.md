## 2. How to Optimize Your Website for Better Performance

**Category:** Performance  
**Date:** 2024-01-10  
**Author:** Michael Chen  
**Read time:** 2 min read  
**URL:** /insights/optimize-website-performance

**Excerpt:** Learn practical tips and techniques to improve your website's loading speed and overall performance for better user experience.

### Content

Performance directly impacts user satisfaction, SEO, and conversion. A slow site costs you visitors, rankings, and revenue. Here we outline proven strategies to make your site faster and more reliable—and how the right hosting and build choices support them. Core Web Vitals Google's Core Web Vitals—LCP (Largest Contentful Paint), INP (Interaction to Next Paint), and CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift)—measure loading, interactivity, and visual stability. They influence both user experience and search rankings. Tools like Lighthouse and PageSpeed Insights give you a clear picture of where you stand and what to improve first. Improvements often come from a combination of server response time, asset size, and render-blocking resources. We prioritise these when we design and build sites, and we can help you interpret your scores and plan targeted fixes. Many of our clients see meaningful gains after optimising images, fonts, and critical path delivery—sometimes in conjunction with a move to our hosting and CDN setup. Asset Optimization Compress images, use modern formats like WebP or AVIF where supported, and lazy-load below-the-fold content. Minimise and bundle JavaScript and CSS to reduce the number of requests and the amount of data transferred. Small decisions—font subsets, deferred non-critical scripts, and efficient markup—add up to noticeably faster loads. If you're on an existing site, start with an audit: identify the heaviest assets and the slowest endpoints. Often the biggest wins come from images (sizing, format, and lazy loading) and from trimming or deferring third-party scripts. Our web design and build process bakes in these practices so new projects ship lean by default. Hosting and CDN Choose a fast host and use a CDN to serve static assets from edge locations close to your users. Caching strategies—for pages, API responses, and static files—can dramatically reduce load times for returning visitors and lower the load on your origin server. We offer managed hosting with performance in mind: solid infrastructure, CDN delivery, and optional support for ongoing tuning. If you're planning a new site or a migration, starting a project with us means we can align architecture and hosting from day one.

Mobile-first also tends to improve performance: you're forced to prioritise what really matters and to keep layouts and assets lean. Broken layouts or unreadable text on mobile can hurt both rankings and trust. We build responsive sites with semantic structure and performance in mind, so you get SEO benefits without sacrificing design. Combined with solid hosting and ongoing support, your site can stay fast and visible as you add content and features. Consistency and Maintainability One codebase that adapts is easier to maintain than separate mobile and desktop sites. Design systems and component-based approaches make responsive layouts more consistent and reduce the risk of one viewport drifting out of sync. ## 4. Web Accessibility: Building Inclusive Digital Experiences

**Category:** Accessibility  
**Date:** 2023-12-28  
**Author:** David Thompson  
**Read time:** 2 min read  
**URL:** /insights/web-accessibility-inclusive-experiences

**Excerpt:** Learn how to make your website accessible to all users, including those with disabilities, and why it's crucial for your business.

### Content

Accessibility is about ensuring everyone can use your website, regardless of ability. It's a legal requirement in many jurisdictions, improves usability for everyone—including people on small screens or slow connections—and reflects a commitment to inclusive design. Getting it right from the start is easier and cheaper than retrofitting later. Our web design process builds in accessibility from the ground up: semantic structure, keyboard support, and clear visual design. We can also advise on audits and ongoing support to keep your site compliant as content and features evolve. Semantic HTML and ARIA Use proper headings, landmarks, and ARIA attributes where needed. Screen readers and other assistive technologies rely on structure to navigate and explain content. A logical heading hierarchy (one h1, then h2, h3 as needed) and regions like nav, main, and footer make it possible for users to jump to the right place without listening to every word. When custom widgets or dynamic content can't be expressed with HTML alone, ARIA can fill the gaps—but use it sparingly and test with real assistive tech. We apply these practices on every project and can help you understand how they affect your content and design choices. If you're starting a new project, we'll scope accessibility into the brief from day one. Keyboard and Focus All interactive elements should be reachable and usable via keyboard. Visible focus indicators help users know where they are on the page and are essential for keyboard and switch users. Avoid removing or weakening focus styles for aesthetic reasons; if you need a custom look, replace the default with something that's at least as visible. Modals, dropdowns, and custom controls need trap focus and sensible tab order so users don't get lost. We build these patterns into our components and test with keyboard-only navigation. For existing sites, we can run a focused audit and recommend fixes. Colour and Contrast Ensure sufficient contrast between text and background (WCAG AA is the common target). Don't rely on colour alone to convey information—use icons, labels, or patterns as well so that colour-blind and low-vision users aren't excluded. Type size and weight also affect readability; we tune these as part of our design system. Accessibility is an ongoing commitment. From initial design and build to content updates and new features, we can help you keep your site inclusive. Get in touch to discuss your next steps.

## 9. Typography on the Web: Choosing Fonts That Work

**Category:** Web Design  
**Date:** 2023-11-28  
**Author:** Michael Chen  
**Read time:** 2 min read  
**URL:** /insights/typography-web-choosing-fonts

**Excerpt:** A practical guide to selecting and pairing typefaces for readability, brand, and performance.

### Content

Typography shapes how your content is read and how your brand is perceived. The right choices improve clarity and trust; the wrong ones can hurt both. On the web, type also affects performance and accessibility—so your font decisions should balance aesthetics with readability and load time. In our web design work we establish type systems early: a small set of typefaces, clear hierarchy, and loading strategy. When you start a project with us, we'll align typography with your brand and technical constraints so your site looks and performs well on every device. Readability First Body text needs to be easy to read at typical sizes and on all devices. Stick to proven serif or sans-serif families with good letterforms and spacing. Avoid novelty or display fonts for long copy; reserve them for headlines or short accents if at all. Line length, line height, and contrast matter as much as the font itself—we tune these in our design system so content stays comfortable to read. Accessibility guidelines recommend minimum contrast and legible font sizes; we build these into our designs and can advise on type choices for users with low vision or dyslexia. Our support includes guidance on keeping typography consistent as you add new pages and templates. Pair With Purpose Limit yourself to two typefaces—one for headings, one for body—and ensure they contrast (e.g. a strong heading font with a neutral body font). Too many fonts create visual noise and add weight. If you need more variety, use weight and style within the same family (e.g. regular and bold, or italic for emphasis) rather than adding new families. We define type scales and pairings in our component library so every page stays consistent. If you're rebranding or refreshing an existing site, we can propose a type system that fits your brand and your hosting and performance budget. Performance and Loading Web fonts add requests and can affect load time and layout shift. Subset fonts when possible (e.g. Latin only if that's all you need), use font-display: swap to avoid invisible text during load, and consider system fonts for a zero-cost, fast option. Self-hosting or a reliable font provider with a good SLA keeps control and performance predictable. If you want typography that supports both brand and performance, get in touch. We'll help you choose and implement fonts that look great and load fast.
